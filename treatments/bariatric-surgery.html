Who May Be Considered

Bariatric surgery is for specific clinical cases.

Surgery is considered when BMI is high enough, related health conditions are present and other methods have failed to produce lasting results.

The assessment process reviews overall health, surgical risk, mental health readiness and long-term follow-up capacity.

01BMI ≥ 35 with related health conditions
02BMI ≥ 40 in some cases without conditions
03Type 2 diabetes related to obesity
04Sleep apnoea and cardiovascular risk
05Failed sustained non-surgical weight loss
06Commitment to long-term follow-up

Surgery is only one part of the journey.

Long-term success after bariatric surgery depends on lifestyle, nutritional support and regular monitoring — not surgery alone.

Before You Book

Questions worth asking.

Clear answers help compare options with confidence.

Am I eligible for bariatric surgery?

Eligibility is based on BMI, related health conditions and medical history. A full assessment is required to determine suitability.

What is the difference between gastric sleeve and bypass?

Sleeve reduces stomach size. Bypass also reroutes the intestine for greater metabolic effect. The right choice depends on your profile.

How much weight will I lose?

Results vary. Success depends on the procedure, adherence to dietary changes and long-term follow-up.

What are the long-term requirements after surgery?

Lifelong nutritional supplementation, regular monitoring and dietary discipline are expected after bariatric procedures.
